Q = (Q1+Q2)/2
  • The formula used when two objects come into contact, touch and seperate again.
  • This formula is used to find the final charge of either object after they seperate.
Q = unit of charge : Coulombs (C)
Q1 = initial charge of object 1
Q2 = initial charge of object 2

n = Q/qe
  • This formula is used when looking for the number of electrons.
  • We can use this to calculate the number of electrons added or removed to give an object charge.
  • Or we can use it to calculate how many electrons were transfered during contact.
n = Number of electrons
Q = Change in charge (Final - Initial)
qe = the charge on an electron (1.60217663 x 10^-19 coulombs)

Charge
  • Neutrally charged : equal protons and electrons and therefore the net charge is 0.
  • Positively charged : more protons than electrons and has a net positive charge (a deficit of electrons).
  • Negatively charged : more electrons than protons (excess of electrons) and has a net negative charge.
An object aquires a charge throught the transfer of electrons (NB - only electrons can be transfered)
  • An object aquires a negative charge by accepting electrons
  • An object aquires a positive charge by Donating electrons
electrons can be rubbed from one object onto another.

Millicoulombs = 1 x 10^-3

Electrical Circuits


Electricity
  • The movement of charge creates electricity.
  • Electricity is essentially the movement or flow of charge(mostly electrons).
  • A battery or another source of electrical energy is connected with electrically conductive wires to electrical components.
  • Materials with high electron mobility (conductors) are used to form pathways for current to flow.
Chemical Potential Energy Electrical Energy Heat/Light Energy
the battery which causes the charge to move arround the circuit otr energy needed to power appliances

  • Although the moving charge in most metal conductors are electrons (which flow from the negative terminal of a battery to its positive terminal), we will work with "conventional current" which is the flow of protons.
  • Conventional current flows from the positive terminal of the battery to the negative terminal

  • For electrical current to flow there needs to be a complete (closed) circuit and something to push it through said circuit.
  • Conductive copper wires form a path for the current to flow through.
  • The push is provided by the battery (cells) or power supply (energy source)

Battery
Batteries consist of one or more cells.
The battery transfers charge into the conductor (NB: a battery does not create charge).
There is a conversion of chemical potential eneergy into electrical energy
Within a circuit this electrical energy is converted into other energy forms such ass heat or light

Resistors
The movement of electrons through a conductor is obstructed by collisions with each other and with the positive cores of atoms - this causes a measure of resistance to the flow of charge.
Resistors are used to control the strength of the current

Rheostats
A variable resistor
The resistance can be increased or decreased as required

Ammeter
An instrument in a circuit that is used to measure the strength of electric current.
Current is measured through amperes (A).
Connected in series

Voltmeter
An instrument that is used to measure the potential difference (voltage) across a component in a circuit.
Potential difference is measured in volts.
Voltmeters are connected in parallel due to their high resistance
